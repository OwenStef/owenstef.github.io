The argument

Political disagreement feels different now.

As predictive systems feed different segments systematically different content, those segments develop incompatible standards for what counts as evidence, sincerity, and normative rightness.

The point is not that people disagree more. It is that the shared ground for contesting disagreements has been eroded. What reads as a conclusive refutation in one segment reads as proof of delusion in another.

I make the case with Habermas’s idea of the lifeworld: the shared meanings and norms that people revise by talking to each other. Then I look at two EU laws as answers: the Digital Services Act, and Regulation (EU) 2024/900 on political ads. Both give people more to see and more ways to opt out. Neither touches the loop underneath, where what people click decides what they see next.

An excerpt

From the thesis. A “validity claim” is the claim any statement makes to be true, right and sincere.

Reasons must be accepted as such across contexts and segments. An appeal to evidence, an accusation of bad faith, or a claim about what is normatively right must be able to reach some shared common space of contestation. Disagreement is organic, and complete agreement is equally problematic, but recognizing validity claims as reasonable is a basic requirement for productive contestation. Once segments begin to diverge at the level of standards, validity claims become locally intelligible but entirely non-transferable. Claims become obvious and uncontestable in one informational environment and entirely incomprehensible in another. What, in one group, can be a conclusive refutation, is seen in another as proof that the out-group is coordinated, corrupt, or delusional.

Why I wrote it

The usual explanations for why political argument had changed pointed at the people doing the arguing. That felt wrong to me. I felt as though the underlying system of communication must also have changed, to allow for the change we were seeing.

The purpose of this project isn’t to solve that division but to provide the language and tools to understand how we got to this place, and then we can begin to think about a path forward.

Where it points

The thesis is about how one technology changed the way we argue. What I want to figure out now is how AI changes the way we function, and how I can help guide that change.
